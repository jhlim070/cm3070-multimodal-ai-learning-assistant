Data Models : Normalisation Tarapong Sreenuch PhD 8 February 2026 10Functional Dependency :What Determines What
In the customers table, customer_id → name means customer 1 is always 'Alice Tan'. This is 
what a primary key does —it determines everything else in the row.
But FDs are not limited to primary keys. In Failure 1, postcode → city is an FD: postcode 
018956 determines city Singapore. The postcode is a determinant —but not the primary key. 
That mismatch is exactly what caused the drift.
Functional dependency (FD): X →Y —if two rows agree on X, they must agree on Y. 
Knowing X uniquely determines Y.

it does, you have a partial dependency —a normal form violation we will name shortly.

Data Models : Normalisation Tarapong Sreenuch PhD 8 February 2026 14Candidate Key
FDs tell us what determines what. The next question: which determinants can serve as the row's 
identifier ?
A candidate key is a minimal set of attributes that uniquely identifies every row. "Minimal" 
means removing any attribute breaks uniqueness.
▪Most tables have one candidate key →becomes the primary key
▪Some have multiple: customers might have both customer_id and email (if emails are 
unique)
▪All candidate keys share one property: they functionally determine every other attribute
Candidate key: a minimal set of attributes whose values uniquely determine every other 
attribute in the table.

Data Models : Normalisation Tarapong Sreenuch PhD 8 February 2026 19BCNF :Every Determinant Is a Key
BCNF is the strictest practical target. The rule: every determinant in the table is a candidate key. If 
a column determines another column, it must be a key —otherwise, you have a second path for 
the same fact to drift.
You do not build tables by walking 1NF →2NF →3NF →BCNF. You design directly to BCNF . 
The ladder exists for diagnosis —naming what is wrong with a legacy table.
Predict: The orders table has order_id , customer_id , order_date , status . Does every 
non-key column describe the thing identified by order_id ?

Data Models : Normalisation Tarapong Sreenuch PhD 8 February 2026 20"One Table, One Fact" :Revealed
Yes. customer_id identifies which customer placed this order . order_date is the date of this 
order . status is the status of this order . Every non -key column describes the order, not something 
else.
Formally: the only determinant ( order_id ) is a candidate key. The table is in BCNF.
The intuitive test: does every non -key column describe the thing identified by the primary key, 
and *only* that thing? If a column describes something else —a postcode describes a city, not a 
customer —it belongs in its own table.
The intuitive test and the formal BCNF definition are two views of the same principle.

Data Models : Normalisation Tarapong Sreenuch PhD 8 February 2026 21Lossless Decomposition :The Safety Net
When you split a table to eliminate redundancy, the join must recover the original data exactly. If 
the join produces extra rows ( spurious tuples ), the decomposition is lossy .
The test: the shared columns must be a candidate key of at least one part.
SAFE:   (student_id, course_id) + (course_id, instructor)
shared: course_id —key of second table ✓
LOSSY:  (student_id, instructor) + (course_id, instructor)
shared: instructor —not a key of either ✗
→ phantom student -course combinations
In practice, decomposing along a functional dependency —which is what the normal form rules 
tell you to do —is always lossless.
The OLTP vocabulary is now complete —FDs, candidate keys, normal forms, and lossless 
decomposition. What about the reporting workload?

Data Models : Normalisation Tarapong Sreenuch PhD 8 February 2026 25Why BCNF Eliminates Truth Drift
If every determinant is a candidate key, every non -key fact has one determination path —
through the key. One path →one copy →no drift.
In Failure 1, postcode determined city but was not a candidate key. That gave city two paths
—and when city also appeared on orders , three places could hold the same fact with nothing 
forcing them to agree.
Decomposing into customers(..., postcode) + postcodes(postcode, city) makes 
postcode the key of its own table. City stored once . The join is the cost; the safety is the benefit. 
BCNF is the design -time discipline that shapes the schema so the engine's runtime constraints 
(PK, FK, UNIQUE) are sufficient.

Each level names a different disease —and each disease has its own cure:

Not 1NF Multi -valued cells, repeating groups Separate table, one row per value

2NF not 3NF Transitive dependency (non -key →non-key) Extract lookup table for the chain
3NF not BCNF Non -key determinant exists Decompose along the violating FD
Without the vocabulary: "this table is bad." With it: "2NF but not 3NF —transitive dependency 
postcode → city —decompose to a lookup table." The diagnosis is the prescription.

Data Models : Normalisation Tarapong Sreenuch PhD 8 February 2026 27Why Full Normalisation Kills Reporting
BCNF distributes facts across many small tables. Each table is clean —but analytical queries 
must reconstruct the picture by joining them back.
Failure 2's "revenue by department by quarter" query:
order_lines → products → categories → departments → orders (for date)
JOIN 1       JOIN 2      JOIN 3        JOIN 4
Four joins across five tables. Each join reconstructs what normalisation deliberately broke apart. 
The join chain is the structural cost of normalisation .
▪OLTP cost: acceptable —transactional queries rarely need all five tables
▪OLAP cost: fatal —every analytical query needs the full picture

Data Models : Normalisation Tarapong Sreenuch PhD 8 February 2026 28Why Star Schema Denormalisation Is Safe
Three reasons the same 3NF violation that caused Failure 1 is safe in a star schema:
Read -only. Dimension tables are not edited by transactional users. No updates →no update 
anomalies. Nobody changes department_name in product_dim directly.
Derived from normalised truth. The star is rebuilt from the BCNF source via ETL. If the source 
changes, the star is regenerated —it is never the primary record.
OLTP (read/write, BCNF) ── ►ETL ──►STAR (read -only, 2NF)
Controlled scope. The violations are named, documented, and bounded —only dimension tables 
contain redundancy.
Predict: A department is renamed from "Home & Garden" to "Home & Living" in the normalised

Data Models : Normalisation Tarapong Sreenuch PhD 8 February 2026 29Truth Is Normalised ,Reporting Is Derived :Revealed
The product_dim must be regenerated from the normalised source. The ETL pipeline rebuilds 
it, picking up the new name automatically.
If someone renamed it directly in product_dim , the star would show "Home & Living" while

The safety argument holds only if the star is always derived, never edited .
If you find yourself editing a dimension table directly —rather than regenerating it from the 
normalised source —you have lost the safety argument. Full stop.

Data Models : Normalisation Tarapong Sreenuch PhD 8 February 2026 33The Algorithm :Design to BCNF, Then Justify
Step 1: Verify each table against BCNF. List FDs, identify candidate keys, check every 
determinant. If a determinant is not a key →decompose.
Step 2: Identify requirements that demand controlled redundancy. Snapshots, derived 
attributes, reporting shapes, dependency preservation.
Step 3: Document each downshift. State the NF relaxed, the requirement, and the safety 
mechanism. An undocumented deviation is indistinguishable from a mistake.
Step 4: Annotate the schema. Mark each table's NF status and downshift type.

Data Models : Normalisation Tarapong Sreenuch PhD 8 February 2026 47Check 1 :Truth -Drift Anomaly (Hospital)
A hospital stores patient city directly on the patients table: (patient_id, name, postcode, 
city) . Two patients share postcode SW1A 1AA . A borough is renamed. A developer updates one 
patient's city but not the other.
SELECT DISTINCT city FROM patients WHERE postcode = 'SW1A 1AA' returns:
Two rows —the old name and the new. Truth has drifted. The FD postcode → city makes 
postcode a determinant that is not a candidate key —a 3NF violation (transitive dependency). 
Decompose: patients( patient_id , name, postcode) + postcodes(postcode, city) . 
Update one row in postcodes , both patients see the correct city.

Data Models : Normalisation Tarapong Sreenuch PhD 8 February 2026 48Check 2 :Snapshot vs Redundancy (Pharmacy)
A pharmacy system stores prescription_address on the dispensing table. A colleague says: 
"The patient's address is already in patients . This is redundant —remove it and join."
Are they correct?
No. The prescription_address records where the medication was delivered at dispensing 
time —a historical fact . If the patient moves, the dispensing record must still show the 
original delivery location. This is a snapshot , not a copy. The technical BCNF violation is safe 
because the data is immutable. Removing it would destroy historical data .

Data Models : Normalisation Tarapong Sreenuch PhD 8 February 2026 49Check 3 :Star Schema NF Identification (Music Streaming)
A music streaming service has artist_dim(artist_key, artist_name, genre_name, 
label_name) . Each genre belongs to one label. Identify the FDs and state the normal form.
FDs: artist_key → artist_name, genre_name, label_name (key determines all). Also: 
genre_name → label_name (each genre belongs to one label). The determinant genre_name is 
not a candidate key. The table is in 2NF but not 3NF —transitive dependency. In the star 
schema: acceptable (read -only, derived).

A tax authority needs "total revenue by industry by fiscal year." The BCNF schema has: filings

Data Models : Normalisation Tarapong Sreenuch PhD 8 February 2026 52Check 5 :Partial Dependency (Ride -Sharing)
A ride -sharing company has trip_assignments(driver_id, vehicle_id, driver_name, 
vehicle_plate, trip_date) with composite PK (driver_id, vehicle_id, trip_date) .
driver_name depends only on driver_id . vehicle_plate depends only on vehicle_id .
What normal form is this table in? What is the violating FD?
1NF but not 2NF. The violating FDs are partial dependencies: driver_id → driver_name and 
vehicle_id → vehicle_plate —non-key attributes depend on part of the composite key, not 
the whole key. Fix: decompose into drivers( driver_id , driver_name ), 
vehicles( vehicle_id , vehicle_plate ), trip_assignments (driver_id , vehicle_id , 
trip_date ).

Data Models : Normalisation Tarapong Sreenuch PhD 8 February 2026 53Check 6 :Cross -Topic: Design -Time vs Runtime
Topic 1 placed the relational model at "engine rejects" on the enforcement spectrum. 
Normalisation adds a design -time discipline on top of the engine's runtime enforcement.
Can the database engine enforce BCNF? Can you write a constraint that says "every 
determinant must be a candidate key"?
No. BCNF is a design principle, not a runtime constraint. The engine enforces the consequences
of BCNF —PRIMARY KEY, FOREIGN KEY, NOT NULL, UNIQUE —but it cannot verify that 
the designer correctly identified all FDs. The engine enforces constraints within a schema; 
normalisation determines the shape of the schema. Two disciplines, two moments —
complementary.
